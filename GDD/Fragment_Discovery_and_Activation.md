# Fragment Discovery and Activation

- **Status:** Provisional foundational concept
- **Related documents:** [PlaneGuardian GDD](PlaneGuardian_GDD.md), [First Plane Onboarding](First_Plane_Onboarding.md), [Card Origins and Protected Discovery](../Procedural/Card_Origins_and_Protected_Discovery.md)

## Purpose

The Void is the shattered, nearly dead remainder of a multiverse. A new
Guardian first hears a whisper: **“Awaken, Guardian.”** The Guardian is then
entrusted with an unsprouted Planar Seed and the work of growing it into a
living plane of reality.

The Seed cannot be grown from nothing. Guardians must retrieve **fragments of
reality**: latent pieces of worlds, histories, beings, places, and forces that
survived the shattering. Each fragment appears as a diverse crystal whose
visual form previews its semantic profile. A fragment is not yet a card. It
becomes a realised card only when the Guardian activates it.

This distinction makes finding and making meaningful actions rather than
instantaneous card delivery:

```text
Guardian intention or lead
  -> locate, retrieve, or quest for a fragment
  -> claim a latent reality crystal
  -> activate the fragment through a ritual, spell, craft, or other process
  -> realise the card
  -> place or otherwise commit the card
```

## Discovery and activation

Actions that previously read as “summon a card” should normally be framed as
locating, retrieving, recovering, or questing for a fragment. A source phrase,
location, divination, formula, or other player input is a lead used by the
protected discovery system; it is not itself the card.

The discovery action may reveal a fragment's semantic profile, visual crystal,
provenance, and activation requirements before the card is realised. The exact
amount of preview information is provisional and should preserve mystery while
making the choice meaningful.

Activation is a separate player decision and authoritative game event. It uses
an appropriate process—such as a ritual, spell, crafted apparatus, sacrifice,
or another card-defined method—and may consume resources, an Action, time, or
cooldown. The process should express the fragment's identity: a Primal
fragment may require a rite, while an Artifice fragment may require a device or
precise construction.

Once activation succeeds, the semantic profile resolves into the deterministic
card definition and the realised card enters the collection or the explicitly
defined destination. Activation must not silently reroll the fragment. Whether
an unactivated fragment can be traded, stored, lost, or abandoned remains an
open rules question.

## Supply and provenance

The protected source mapping still prevents offline mining and keeps a shared
lead stable within its Era. The intended lifecycle is:

- locating a fragment records the discovery lead and creates or claims the
  latent fragment instance;
- activation resolves the fragment's deterministic card and records the
  activation process;
- global supply, account limits, ownership, and provenance remain
  authoritative and atomic.

The precise point at which a limited-copy reservation becomes irreversible is
provisional, but the system must not allow activation to bypass supply limits
or create duplicate unique cards.

## Narrative and presentation contract

The first session should establish this vocabulary in order:

1. the whisper awakens the Guardian;
2. the Guardian sees the ruined Void;
3. an unsprouted Seed is revealed and entrusted to the Guardian;
4. the Guardian learns that reality can be rebuilt from fragments;
5. the first fragments are located and activated;
6. the resulting cards begin the Plane.

“Summoning” remains valid when a specific card or effect truly manifests
something, but it is no longer the default term for finding a card. UI labels,
Action names, onboarding text, and art direction should prefer **fragment**,
**locate**, **retrieve**, **quest**, **activate**, and **realise** where this
distinction matters.

## Open questions

- How much of a fragment's semantic profile and future card can be previewed?
- Which activation processes and resource costs are common, rare, or card-specific?
- Can latent fragments be traded, stored across Eras, or lost before activation?
- Does failed activation consume the fragment, the activation cost, or neither?
- Is the first Seed itself a fragment, or a distinct gift from the surviving
  multiverse?
