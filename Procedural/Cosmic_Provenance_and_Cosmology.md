# Cosmic Provenance and Cosmology

- **Status:** Foundational design with structural guidelines
- **Related decision:** [DD-0013](../Decisions/DD-0013-cosmic-provenance-and-cosmology.md)
- **Related documents:** [Lore Map and Affinity Wheels](Lore_Map_and_Affinity_Wheels.md), [PlaneGuardian GDD](../GDD/PlaneGuardian_GDD.md), [DD-0004 — Universes, Eras, and Eternal Cards](../Decisions/DD-0004-universes-eras-and-eternal-cards.md)

## Purpose

Cosmic Provenance describes the greater authority, realm, or lineage from
which a card derives identity.

> Whose greater order, dominion, or cosmological lineage claims this card?

```text
Divine <-> Infernal
```

The axis is a common semantic spine that opens into two asymmetric generated
structures: a relational pantheon and a hierarchical Hell.

## Provenance is not morality

- **Divine** provenance derives from godly identity, sacred office, worship,
  covenant, revelation, blessing, consecration, or divine lineage.
- **Infernal** provenance derives from infernal rank, dominion, binding claim,
  command, obligation, service, conquest, or infernal lineage.

Divinity tends to recognize authority through cosmic office, worship,
responsibility, covenant, or acknowledged place in the pantheon. Infernal
authority tends to operate through rank, possession, enforceable claim,
service, and the power to command or punish.

These are tendencies of structure rather than moral judgments. A tyrannical
god remains Divine. An infernal governor may be honorable, protective, and
exact in every bargain. Ethos determines values and World Relation describes
how an entity acts upon reality.

## The exact center — Worldly or Unaligned

The center represents provenance independent of both Heaven and Hell. It may
include mortal, natural, arcane, self-wrought, unaffiliated, or sovereign
identities.

- **Low Salience near the center:** cosmic provenance is irrelevant.
- **High Salience near the center:** being unclaimed, self-determined, or
  resistant to cosmic authority is central to the card.

Possible high-Salience vocabulary includes *World-Born*, *Unclaimed*,
*Self-Wrought*, *Unbeholden*, *Godless*, *Free*, *Mortal*, and *Sovereign*.

Worldly is distinct from the Mundane center of Magical Tradition. An unaligned
wizard may be Worldly and Arcane. A divine artisan may be Divine and Mundane.
An infernal engineer may be Infernal and strongly aligned with Artifice.

## Universe-seeded cosmology

The Universe seed determines the hidden generation inputs for:

- gods and major infernals;
- divine offices and infernal ranks;
- domains, symbols, names, and presentation grammar;
- alliances, rivalries, lineages, and chains of command;
- religions, cults, institutions, and factions;
- cosmological association rules and historical tensions.

The generator remains versioned and deterministic. An Era may alter mortal
history and occupancy without necessarily rebuilding the cosmology; the exact
reset boundary remains future work.

## Gods and infernals as semantic attractors

A cosmological entity possesses an unusually strong and coherent Lore Map
profile. It should normally have:

- two or three defining affinities;
- one dominant opposition, contradiction, or tension;
- several secondary tendencies;
- a characteristic Magical Tradition and Manifestation;
- an office, domain, jurisdiction, or claim;
- signature mechanics, vocabulary, forms, symbols, and materials;
- explicit relationships to other cosmological entities.

Not every wheel must be extreme. Concentrating identity in selected wheels
creates clearer gods and infernals than maximizing every coordinate.

The foundational rule is:

> Gods and infernals do not define the Lore Map. They are generated as
> powerful inhabitants of it.

## The relational pantheon

The pantheon is generated as a wheel or network of relationships rather than
a hierarchy with one universal chain of command. A god is defined partly by
what it contests, complements, protects, inherits, and shares.

Pantheon relationships may include:

- opposition;
- alliance;
- kinship or descent;
- rivalry;
- shared or disputed office;
- overlapping domain;
- succession;
- marriage, oath, or covenant;
- exile or reconciliation.

### Opposition wheel

Divine oppositions derive from strong contrasts in the existing Lore Map.
They are not fixed universal pairs and are not necessarily moral conflicts.
A Universe may emphasize preservation against transformation, order against
chaos, rooted stewardship against wandering revelation, or ritual observance
against instinctive inspiration.

Opposition should produce a meaningful theological disagreement rather than
an inverted copy. Two gods may both favor Life while disagreeing over whether
life flourishes through protected cultivation or migration and adaptation.

### Illustrative opposition

A hypothetical **God of Walled Gardens** might be strongly Order-aligned,
Life-aligned, Preservation-oriented, Rooted, Embodied, Ritual-oriented, and
associated with Earth and Water.

Its rival, a hypothetical **Goddess of Migrating Seeds**, might be moderately
Chaos-aligned, strongly Life-aligned, Transformation-oriented, Wandering,
partly Incorporeal, Wilding-oriented, and associated with Air and Water.

Both protect life. Their opposition concerns what life requires. These are
illustrations of the generator's intended output, not canonical deities.

## Hierarchical Hell

Hell is generated as a directed authority graph. Rank, allegiance, command,
obligation, succession, and rebellion are part of an infernal's identity.

The hierarchy may contain structural roles such as:

1. sovereign powers;
2. throne regents;
3. dominion lords;
4. ministers or legates;
5. commanders;
6. named servitors;
7. infernal hosts.

These are provisional structural examples. Exact titles and depth should vary
by Universe and its naming cultures.

### Inheritance and specialization

An infernal inherits some semantic influence from its superiors and lineage,
then adds a narrower office, method, contradiction, or ambition. This creates
recognizable infernal families without making every subordinate identical.

A Sovereign of Perfect Dominion might establish Order, Preservation,
Controlled practice, and binding authority. Subordinates might specialize as
a Regent of Unbroken Contracts, a Warden of Captive Names, or a rebellious
Prince of Necessary Betrayal. These are illustrative rather than canonical.

### Hierarchical change

The authority graph supports procedural history:

- promotion after a superior falls;
- demotion after failure;
- succession conflict around an empty office;
- incompatible service to multiple superiors;
- usurpation and disputed rank;
- disgraced infernals retaining unrecognized titles;
- card associations changing when authority changes.

Whether these changes can occur during an Era or only appear as generated
history remains an open question.

## Association and influence

A cosmologically associated card combines:

1. its own deterministic semantic profile;
2. card-family and context relevance;
3. the associated entity's profile;
4. association type and strength;
5. Complexity available to express harmony or tension.

The associated entity biases the card rather than replacing its identity.
Weak association may contribute a symbol, naming fragment, or mechanical
motif. Direct manifestation, lineage, or creation may transmit much more of
the entity's profile.

### Association types

| Relationship | Typical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Consecrated to | Consciously dedicated to an entity or office |
| Blessed by | Receives favor without complete belonging |
| Created by | Intentionally made by the entity |
| Descended from | Inherits cosmological nature or claim |
| Herald of | Represents authority or intent |
| Bound to | Held by covenant, contract, rank, or obligation |
| Serves | Occupies a recognized subordinate relationship |
| Opposed to | Defined partly through resistance |
| Forsaken by | Retains traces of severed influence |
| Contested by | Subject to incompatible claims |
| Usurping | Attempts to seize domain, office, or rank |

Association metadata is a relationship layer, not another affinity wheel.

### Influence discipline

- Preserve the card's own core identity.
- Inherit only concepts relevant to the card family and association type.
- Use association strength to limit semantic displacement.
- Spend Complexity when patron influence creates contradiction.
- Express influence consistently across mechanics, names, art, and lore.
- Allow opposition and rejection to transmit recognizable negative space
  without turning the card into a loyal follower.

## Card-type interpretation

| Card type | Cosmic Provenance primarily describes |
| --- | --- |
| Plane Seed | Cosmic claim, protection, independence, or contested sovereignty |
| Land | Sacred domain, infernal holding, worldly territory, or disputed ground |
| Structure | Temple, office, embassy, fortress, contract site, or free institution |
| Facility | Service to a cult, hierarchy, worldly craft, or cosmological economy |
| Creature | Lineage, blessing, corruption, creation, service, or independence |
| Hero | Patronage, oath, descent, rebellion, or refusal of cosmic claim |
| Leader | Religious office, infernal rank, worldly mandate, or contested authority |
| Squad | Host, order, cult, legion, militia, or unaffiliated company |
| Relic | Consecration, ownership, authorship, claim, or stolen authority |
| Action | Invocation, command, bargain, intercession, defiance, or mortal agency |
| Battle Event | Intervention, judgment, infernal order, broken contract, or worldly event |

## Naming and flavor guidance

Cosmic Provenance should usually contribute claims, offices, relationships,
honorifics, jurisdictions, and institutional vocabulary.

- **Divine:** hallowed, consecrated, blessed, sainted, revealed, anointed,
  covenant, temple, oracle, herald, votary, office, grace.
- **Infernal:** bound, claimed, enthroned, indebted, appointed, forfeit,
  contract, dominion, rank, legate, minister, host, seal.
- **Worldly:** unclaimed, self-wrought, mortal, free, unbeholden, common,
  guild, council, household, company, oath of equals.

Actual vocabulary must be filtered through the generated culture, religion,
entity, office, and association. Generic Divine or Infernal words should not
erase Universe-specific identity.

## Removal test

Removing Cosmic Provenance must leave questions that other wheels cannot
answer:

- Does a greater realm, office, lineage, or authority claim the card?
- Which cosmological entity or hierarchy influences it, and through what
  relationship?
- Is its independence from Heaven and Hell important?
- Does its authority come through worship and office, rank and claim, or
  worldly self-determination?
- Which symbols, titles, institutions, allies, and rivals follow from that
  provenance?

If Ethos determines Divine or Infernal identity automatically, provenance has
collapsed into morality. If Magical Tradition determines it, method has been
confused with authority.

## Open questions

- Pantheon size, topology, opposition strength, and domain-overlap rules.
- Infernal rank depth, title grammars, and authority inheritance.
- Whether Heaven also contains limited offices or Hell contains peer-level
  councils without weakening their primary structural contrast.
- How religions, cults, factions, and schisms derive from cosmological entities.
- Whether players can alter divine offices, infernal succession, or patronage.
- Which cosmological facts survive Era resets and how Hall-of-Fame history
  records exceptional gods, infernals, cults, and rebellions.
- Exact influence functions, association limits, and Complexity costs.
