# World Relation Wheel and Flavor

- **Status:** Foundational design with flavor guidelines
- **Related decision:** [DD-0010](../Decisions/DD-0010-world-relation-wheel.md)
- **Related documents:** [Lore Map and Affinity Wheels](Lore_Map_and_Affinity_Wheels.md), [PlaneGuardian GDD](../GDD/PlaneGuardian_GDD.md)

## Purpose

World Relation describes what a card does to the continued existence and identity of things. Ethos supplies values, Elemental Affinity supplies substance and imagery, and World Relation supplies verbs, processes, and consequences.

The wheel must communicate whether an identity is retained or changed, whether something enters or leaves existence, and whether the process is careful, generative, surgical, or catastrophic. It does not independently imply morality.

## Axes

```text
Preservation <-> Transformation
Creation     <-> Annihilation
```

- **Preservation:** maintain identity, continuity, memory, form, or state.
- **Transformation:** alter identity, function, structure, or state.
- **Creation:** bring something new into existence.
- **Annihilation:** remove something from existence rather than merely damaging or killing it.

Death and Annihilation are distinct. Death may belong to a natural, spiritual, or historical cycle; Annihilation is erasure. Destruction is also not automatically Annihilation because broken things may remain, transform, or be rebuilt.

## Primary regions

### Preservation + Creation — Restoration

Creates by recovering, repeating, or extending an existing pattern.

- **Concepts:** renewal, reconstruction, reconstitution, continuity, replication, recovery, inheritance.
- **Verbs:** restore, renew, rebuild, regrow, reconstitute, mend, recover, return, replicate, rekindle.
- **Images:** pattern, seed, memory, blueprint, echo, cradle, archive, foundation, lineage.
- **Roles:** Restorer, Rebuilder, Patternkeeper, Mender, Renewer, Conservator.
- **Intensity:** Mend → Restore → Reconstitute → Recreate → Return from Oblivion.

Restoration is not limited to Life. It may rebuild a fortress, recreate an extinct landscape, restore an undead lineage, or replicate a construct.

### Transformation + Creation — Genesis

Creates something genuinely new by changing materials, identities, or possibilities.

- **Concepts:** invention, becoming, evolution, forging, metamorphosis, transmutation, emergence.
- **Verbs:** forge, shape, weave, transmute, evolve, awaken, distill, recast, invent, manifest.
- **Images:** crucible, loom, forge, cocoon, workshop, chrysalis, vessel, unwritten pattern.
- **Roles:** Shaper, Forger, Transmuter, Worldsmith, Weaver, Formwright.
- **Intensity:** Shape → Recast → Transmute → Metamorphose → Create the Unprecedented.

This region naturally supports alchemy, artifice, evolution, and resource conversion into new forms.

### Preservation + Annihilation — Excision

Removes something in order to preserve an identity, boundary, structure, or greater whole.

- **Concepts:** excision, quarantine, culling, purification, cauterization, severance, containment, sacrifice.
- **Verbs:** excise, sever, cauterize, purge, cull, prune, quarantine, nullify, contain, forbid.
- **Images:** scalpel, boundary, seal, circle, ward, firebreak, last wall, forbidden name.
- **Roles:** Excisor, Purifier, Boundary Warden, Cauterist, Keeper of the Seal, Last Physician.
- **Intensity:** Separate → Prune → Excise → Nullify → Erase for the Greater Whole.

Excision often suggests precision and intent. Its constructive expression removes a threat to preserve the whole; its oppressive expression erases anything that threatens a preferred pattern.

### Transformation + Annihilation — Dissolution

Destroys identity through breakdown, consumption, conversion, or unmaking.

- **Concepts:** dissolution, unmaking, entropy, consumption, erosion, decomposition, reduction, oblivion.
- **Verbs:** dissolve, consume, unravel, erode, reduce, scatter, decompose, unmake, void, erase.
- **Images:** maw, solvent, ruin, dust, fracture, unraveled thread, empty name, final ash.
- **Roles:** Dissolver, Unmaker, Form-Eater, World-Eroder, Name-Devourer, Weaver of Absence.
- **Intensity:** Wear → Erode → Dissolve → Unmake → Remove from Existence.

Dissolution need not be chaotic. It may be methodical, natural, industrial, or used to feed later creation.

## Axis-centered flavor

| Region | Concepts and verbs | Typical roles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preservation | anchor, ward, archive, remember, stabilize, sustain, endure | Archivist, Warden, Keeper, Stabilizer |
| Transformation | alter, convert, turn, shift, adapt, exchange, rewrite, molt | Shifter, Transmuter, Reformer, Changebearer |
| Creation | summon, manifest, form, build, conjure, kindle, originate | Maker, Summoner, Originator, Genesis-Bearer |
| Annihilation | erase, nullify, unmake, extinguish, void, cancel, remove | Nullifier, Eraser, Ender, Bearer of Final Absence |

Summoning requires mechanical care: it may create a manifestation or merely transport an existing being.

## Center and relationships

A low-Salience center means the card does not meaningfully affect identity or existence. A high-Salience center may represent Conservation: transfer, reflection, substitution, redirection, or equal exchange with no net ontological change.

Possible centered vocabulary includes Equivalence, Conservation, Equal Measure, Redirection, Reflection, Reversal, and Balance of Forms. Titles include Keeper of Equal Measure, Conservator of Forms, and Warden of the Zero Sum.

| Relationship | Flavor direction |
| --- | --- |
| Equilibrium | Conservation, equal exchange, maintained proportions |
| Synthesis | Creation through destruction, rebuilding through transformation |
| Conflict | A thing simultaneously preserving and erasing itself |
| Alternation | Cycles of construction and collapse |
| Suppression | Contained genesis, imprisoned transformation, sealed oblivion |
| Paradox | Creating absence, preserving change, annihilating annihilation |

These special relationships require enough Complexity for mechanics, names, art, and lore to explain the tension.

## Semantic boundaries

- Healing usually combines Restoration with Life, but structures and undead can also be restored.
- Corruption is not synonymous with Transformation; it requires a value judgment from Ethos, culture, or an observer.
- Decay is not automatically Annihilation because ordinary decay transforms and recycles matter.
- Death is not Annihilation.
- Destruction is not necessarily Annihilation.
- Summoning is not necessarily Creation.
- Resurrection may combine Restoration with Death Ethos rather than Life.
- Sacrifice describes a cost or method; its World Relation depends on what it accomplishes.
- Void does not automatically imply evil, Shadow, or Annihilation.

The guiding flavor rule is:

> Preservation remembers. Transformation reshapes. Creation begins. Annihilation removes.

- Mechanical thresholds separating destruction, transformation, and true Annihilation.
- How Restoration recreates cards or entities without bypassing rarity and supply rules.
