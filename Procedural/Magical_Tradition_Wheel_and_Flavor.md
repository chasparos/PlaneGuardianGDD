# Magical Tradition Wheel and Flavor

- **Status:** Foundational design with flavor guidelines
- **Related decision:** [DD-0012](../Decisions/DD-0012-magical-tradition-wheel.md)
- **Related documents:** [Lore Map and Affinity Wheels](Lore_Map_and_Affinity_Wheels.md), [PlaneGuardian GDD](../GDD/PlaneGuardian_GDD.md)

## Purpose

Magical Tradition describes the method by which a card's effect is achieved:
how its power is learned, shaped, practiced, or replaced by ordinary effort.
It does not describe the power's element, purpose, morality, result, or mode of
physical presence.

> By what discipline, tradition, instinct, or effort is this accomplished?

```text
Arcane     <-> Primal
Controlled <-> Instinctive
```

## Axes

### Arcane–Primal

This axis describes the source and language of knowledge.

- **Arcane:** abstract, symbolic, theoretical, named, measured, or constructed
  knowledge; reality is understood through formulae, patterns, diagrams,
  classifications, and deliberately assembled systems.
- **Primal:** living, ancestral, ecological, embodied, or inherited knowledge;
  reality is understood through relationship with land, spirits, beasts,
  seasons, bloodlines, and old customs.

Arcane does not require Control, and Primal does not require Instinct.

### Controlled–Instinctive

This axis describes how a method is expressed.

- **Controlled:** prepared, repeatable, constrained, disciplined, and executed
  according to a known procedure.
- **Instinctive:** immediate, intuitive, adaptive, emotional, spontaneous, or
  responsive to circumstances.

Controlled does not mean safe, and Instinctive does not mean ignorant.

## Primary regions

### Arcane + Controlled — Artifice

Magic treated as an exact, reproducible technology.

- **Concepts:** formula, lattice, theorem, cipher, apparatus, engine, matrix,
  geometry, calibration, containment.
- **Verbs and modifiers:** inscribed, calculated, articulated, sigiled,
  calibrated, encoded, distilled, assembled.
- **Practitioners and instruments:** artificer, runesmith, architect, alchemist,
  diagram, lens, engine, array, construct.
- **Failure flavor:** miscalculation, overload, broken containment, corrupted
  notation, structural defect.
- **Mechanical tendencies:** predictable output, deliberate preparation,
  resource conversion, stored charges, constructed synergies.

Example names include *Calibrated Ember Lattice*, *Architect of Sealed
Angles*, *Runebound Siege Engine*, and *Theorem of Sudden Passage*.

### Arcane + Instinctive — Sorcery

Abstract magic grasped directly through talent, will, intuition, or dangerous
inspiration.

- **Concepts:** impulse, vision, surge, spark, revelation, resonance, will,
  inspiration, improvisation.
- **Verbs and modifiers:** awakened, flashing, will-born, spontaneous,
  unbidden, volatile, incandescent, whispered.
- **Practitioners and instruments:** sorcerer, seer, willshaper, prodigy,
  thought, voice, gesture, gaze.
- **Failure flavor:** backlash, emotional excess, lost concentration, runaway
  resonance, power exceeding intent.
- **Mechanical tendencies:** flexible targets, reactive effects, variable
  output, conditional amplification, rapid expression.

Example names include *Unbidden Flame*, *The Thought That Became Lightning*,
*Willshaper of the Azure Rift*, and *Sudden Transposition*.

### Primal + Controlled — Ritual

Power approached through inherited forms, obligations, ceremony, and correct
relationship.

- **Concepts:** covenant, offering, procession, observance, circle, vigil,
  taboo, tradition, oath, season.
- **Verbs and modifiers:** ancestral, consecrated, seasonal, hallowed, blooded,
  chanted, masked, communal, votive.
- **Practitioners and instruments:** elder, keeper, celebrant, ancestor,
  altar, mask, drum, token, sacred boundary.
- **Failure flavor:** broken taboo, omitted step, unpaid obligation, unsuitable
  season, offended spirit or community.
- **Mechanical tendencies:** timed triggers, communal scaling, recurring
  cycles, offerings, covenants, location or season requirements.

Example names include *Vigil of the First Harvest*, *The Antlered Covenant*,
*Keeper of the Ashen Offering*, and *Rite of Returning Waters*.

### Primal + Instinctive — Wilding

Power expressed as living impulse through hunger, fear, growth, weather,
territorial instinct, rage, migration, or adaptation.

- **Concepts:** roar, hunt, frenzy, molt, eruption, stampede, awakening,
  bloom, hunger, migration.
- **Verbs and modifiers:** feral, untamed, ravenous, surging, bristling,
  blood-hot, storm-driven, blooming, prowling.
- **Practitioners and instruments:** beast, brood, storm, thicket, predator,
  claw, fang, root, breath, instinct.
- **Failure flavor:** exhaustion, panic, misdirected aggression, uncontrolled
  growth, or nature pursuing its own needs.
- **Mechanical tendencies:** adaptation, escalation, emotional or damage
  triggers, environmental response, volatile growth.

Example names include *Thornwake*, *The Forest Answers in Claws*, *Stormblood
Matriarch*, and *Ravenous Spring*.

## The exact center — Mundane

The center is not generic or balanced magic. It is work performed without a
supernatural shortcut. Someone cultivates the field, hauls the stone, drills
the formation, tends the wound, repairs the engine, or walks the distance.

Mundane flavor should be tactile and earned:

- **Concepts:** labor, craft, muscle, patience, practice, logistics,
  engineering, agriculture, medicine, training, endurance, repetition.
- **Verbs and modifiers:** wrought, hewn, tempered, drilled, hauled, raised,
  harvested, repaired, provisioned, fortified, patient, weathered.
- **People and instruments:** mason, smith, surgeon, quartermaster, farmer,
  caravan, workshop, scaffold, hammer, furrow, levy, watch.
- **Failure flavor:** fatigue, delay, injury, weather, poor supply, broken
  equipment, insufficient labor, or human error.

Example names include *Raised by Hand*, *The Long Harvest*, *Stone by Patient
Stone*, *Veteran Riftwatch*, *Twelve Days' March*, *The Mason's Answer*, *No
Miracle Required*, and *Honest Steel*.

Mundane is an affirmative high-fantasy identity. A master smith may forge a
god-killing weapon without personally using magic; a disciplined army may
defeat supernatural creatures through preparation; thousands of workers may
raise a fortress stronger than a conjured wall.

### Salience at the center

- **Low Salience near the center:** how the effect is achieved is ordinary or
  irrelevant to the card.
- **High Salience near the center:** mundane accomplishment, rejection of
  magical shortcuts, or victory through effort is central to the identity.

This distinction prevents a generic road and a celebrated guild of master
road-builders from receiving the same narrative weight.

### Mechanical tendency

Mundane effects tend to use fewer magical or exotic resources while requiring
more time to complete or longer cooldowns before their people, tools, and
facilities are ready again.

> Magic compresses effort into resources. The Mundane pays more of its cost in
> labor and time.

This is a balancing tendency, not a mandatory formula. Specific card types and
abilities may justify exceptions.

## Axis-centered flavor

| Region | Concepts |
| --- | --- |
| Arcane | secret, symbol, theory, name, pattern, occult knowledge |
| Primal | ancestry, ecology, spirit, season, blood, living memory |
| Controlled | discipline, preparation, repetition, procedure, restraint |
| Instinctive | intuition, reflex, improvisation, emotion, response |

A Controlled card near the mundane center may represent military drill,
bureaucracy, surgery, or master craftsmanship. An Instinctive card near the
center may represent reflex, improvisation, street wisdom, or animal handling
without becoming magical.

## Optional Focus

Ordinary cards need no Focus. Complex or strongly centered cards may emphasize:

- **Knowledge:** Arcane--Primal source and language;
- **Practice:** Controlled--Instinctive expression;
- **Craft:** skilled work through tools and trained hands;
- **Discipline:** procedure, organization, endurance, and self-mastery;
- **Ingenuity:** solving extraordinary problems through ordinary means;
- **Industry:** coordinated labor, infrastructure, and production;
- **Defiance:** deliberate rejection of supernatural assistance;
- **Holistic:** source and expression form one inseparable tradition.

Focus constrains exceptional interpretation rather than creating normal
independent naming branches.

## Card-type interpretation

| Card type | Magical Tradition primarily describes |
| --- | --- |
| Plane Seed | How the Plane is stabilized, cultivated, or maintained |
| Land | How its resources are accessed and its environment managed |
| Structure | Whether it is built, inscribed, grown by rite, or manifested |
| Facility | Its operating practice, workforce, tools, and magical method |
| Creature | Whether power is trained, constructed, inherited, or instinctive |
| Hero | Learned discipline, innate talent, tradition, instinct, or mundane mastery |
| Leader | Command method, doctrine, inspiration, ceremony, or logistical skill |
| Squad | Training, equipment, magical support, custom, or collective instinct |
| Relic | How it was made, awakened, used, or maintained |
| Action | The method and price of intervention |
| Battle Event | How an occurrence is prepared, invoked, triggered, or physically performed |

## Constant-outcome test

Holding the result constant should still produce distinct methods:

| Tradition | A wall is created by... |
| --- | --- |
| Artifice | geometric runes assembling or reinforcing it |
| Sorcery | a geomancer shaping it through immediate will |
| Ritual | an ancestral stone rite performed in the proper form |
| Wilding | an enraged earth-spirit erupting from the ground |
| Mundane | masons, tools, materials, and sustained labor |

The same test applies to healing: restoration formula, intuitive sorcery,
herbal rite, regenerative instinct, or surgery, bandaging, and rest.

## Naming guidance

Tradition should usually contribute verbs, instruments, procedures, and
failure language rather than the subject of a name. Other wheels can supply
the substance, intention, change, or presence.

Holding Fire constant can produce:

- **Artifice:** *Inscribed Flame*;
- **Sorcery:** *Will-Born Flame*;
- **Ritual:** *Flame of the Ancestral Vigil*;
- **Wilding:** *Hunting Flame*;
- **Mundane:** *The Charcoal-Burner's Fire*.

## Removal test

Removing Magical Tradition must leave questions that other wheels cannot
answer:

- Was the effect calculated, inherited, improvised, invoked, or labored into
  existence?
- What kind of practitioner, tool, or procedure produced it?
- What does failure look like?
- Does the effect primarily pay through rare resources, preparation, risk,
  labor, or time?
- Is mundanity irrelevant, or is doing it the hard way central to the card?

If Elemental Affinity, card type, or World Relation answers these
automatically, those systems have absorbed Magical Tradition's responsibility.

The guiding rule is:

> Other wheels describe what power is and what it does. Magical Tradition
> describes how the work is performed.

## Open questions

- Exact resource and cooldown biases for each region.
- Whether workforce or labor capacity becomes an explicit Plane resource.
- Cultural variations in ritual, artifice, and mundane vocabulary.
- Whether Wilding remains the final player-facing name for its region.
- How mixed traditions share tools, practitioners, and failure modes.
