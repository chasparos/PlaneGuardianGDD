# DD-0013 — Cosmic Provenance and Cosmology

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-01
- **Affected documents:** [PlaneGuardian GDD](../GDD/PlaneGuardian_GDD.md), [Lore Map and Affinity Wheels](../Procedural/Lore_Map_and_Affinity_Wheels.md), [Cosmic Provenance and Cosmology](../Procedural/Cosmic_Provenance_and_Cosmology.md)

## Context

Cosmic Provenance must distinguish Divine and Infernal association without
reducing them to good and evil. The game also needs gods and infernals with
strong identities that can influence associated cards while remaining
procedurally generated from the Universe seed.

The two cosmic realms should not feel like mirrored factions. A pantheon is
defined through relationships, domains, offices, and opposition, while Hell is
defined through rank, command, obligation, inheritance, and rebellion.

## Decision

Cosmic Provenance uses a **Divine--Infernal** axis. It describes the greater
realm, authority, or lineage from which a card derives identity, not its
morality. Its exact center is **Worldly or Unaligned** provenance: identity
independent of both Heaven and Hell.

Each Universe seed generates two asymmetric cosmological structures:

- a **relational pantheon** whose gods occupy strong Lore Map profiles and are
  connected through opposition, alliance, kinship, rivalry, office, and
  overlapping domain;
- a **hierarchical Hell** whose infernals occupy ranks and lineages through
  command, service, contract, inheritance, specialization, succession,
  demotion, and usurpation.

Gods and infernals are generated as powerful inhabitants of the established
Lore Map. They do not create new meanings for its wheels. Associated cards
retain their own deterministic profiles and receive relationship-weighted
influence from cosmological patrons, creators, superiors, rivals, or lineages.

## Rationale

Structural asymmetry gives Heaven and Hell distinct narrative character while
the common provenance axis keeps card generation comprehensible. Strong Lore
Map profiles make cosmological entities coherent semantic attractors. Using
the Universe seed permits seasonal and Era cosmologies without hard-coding one
canonical pantheon.

The Worldly center gives independent, mortal, natural, arcane, and self-wrought
identities positive meaning. High Salience can make refusal of cosmic claim a
defining trait rather than an absence of content.

## Consequences

- Divine does not imply benevolence, and Infernal does not imply cruelty or
  dishonor.
- Pantheon generation needs relationship and opposition constraints; infernal
  generation needs a rank and authority graph.
- Association type and strength determine which parts of a cosmological
  entity's profile may influence a card.
- Gods, infernals, religions, titles, symbols, and cultural vocabulary remain
  Universe-generated content rather than fixed universal lore.
- Exact pantheon size, infernal ranks, succession rules, Era persistence, and
  player interaction with cosmological offices remain open design work.
